\subsection{Conservative Charging Admission}
\label{app:conservative_admission}
At each station, fix a nonoverlapping per-plug calendar containing all
vehicles currently charging and all committed inbound vehicles. Process
all current potential candidates in increasing $(a_{k,c,t},k)$ order.
Place each virtual interval
$[\sigma_{k,c,t},\sigma_{k,c,t}+h_{k,c,t})$ in the earliest complete gap
at or after $a_{k,c,t}$, following earlier virtual jobs on that plug
and avoiding fixed reservations. Even
candidates eventually rejected retain their virtual intervals until
construction is complete. The mask in~\eqref{eq:conservative_mask}
applies in addition to the usual reachability and battery checks.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:conservative_queue_safety}]
By construction, the virtual intervals and existing reservations do
not overlap on any plug. The optimizer selects a subset of the virtual
candidates; deleting unselected intervals cannot create an overlap.
For each selected edge, $\sigma_{k,c,t}=a_{k,c,t}$, so its virtual
interval is exactly its arrival-to-completion interval
$[a_{k,c,t},a_{k,c,t}+h_{k,c,t})$. It therefore starts charging on
arrival and leaves every existing reservation unchanged. Only selected
intervals are added to the real calendar. Starting from a queue-free
calendar, the same argument applies at subsequent epochs if all later
charging arrivals obey this rule, selected reservations are honored,
and releases precede simultaneous arrivals. Hence the queue-free
property is preserved inductively over feasible decision epochs.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Reactive comparison.}
Reactive admission checks candidates against existing commitments,
including inbound vehicles, without inserting all current potential
arrivals into a virtual calendar. These individual checks alone do not
certify joint interval feasibility; queueing, if any, is handled by the
simulator. This is an operational comparison, rather than an alternative
queue-free setting for the proposition. Neither policy reserves capacity
for unknown charging decisions at future epochs. The conservative rule
may leave capacity unused because one vehicle can appear in several
stations' virtual calendars but selects at most one charging action.
