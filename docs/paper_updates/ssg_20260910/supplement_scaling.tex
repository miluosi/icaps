\subsection{Exact Assignment--Flow Equivalence}
\label{app:proof_flow}
Let $\mathcal K$ be the current decision vehicles and $\mathcal A$ the
encoded action resources other than separately supplied private
fallbacks. Every feasible edge $(k,a)$ has a fixed,
finite score $\Psi_{k,a}$ and each resource has integer capacity $u_a$.
An explicitly supplied private outside action $b_k^0$, if present, is
available only to vehicle $k$ and is distinct from $\mathcal A$.
Let $\mathcal P$ collect these private resources. Include their edges
and the feasible edges to $\mathcal A$ in $\mathcal E_K$;
do not insert an edge when no such physical action is admissible.
The complete assignment problem is
\begin{align}
J_F^\star=\max_z\quad&\sum_{(k,a)\in\mathcal E_K}\Psi_{k,a}z_{k,a}
\label{eq:appendix_assignment_obj}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad&\sum_{a:(k,a)\in\mathcal E_K}z_{k,a}=1
&&\forall k,\label{eq:appendix_assignment_vehicle}\\
&\sum_{k:(k,a)\in\mathcal E_K}z_{k,a}\le u_a
&&\forall a\in\mathcal A\cup\mathcal P,\label{eq:appendix_assignment_capacity}\\
&z_{k,a}\in\{0,1\}&&\forall(k,a)\in\mathcal E_K.
\label{eq:appendix_assignment_binary}
\end{align}
Private resources have capacity one. Energy and pickup restrictions
are edgewise, and a station edge consumes exactly one resource quota
under Assumption~\ref{ass:reservation}. There is no additional
cross-resource selector constraint. Remove zero-capacity edges before
reduction; clipping a resource capacity to its feasible incidence
degree also preserves this model.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ilp-mcf}]
Given a feasible assignment, send one unit from $o$ through each
vehicle to its chosen action, and then to $d$. Conservation follows
from the exactly-one constraints and action--sink capacities enforce
the resource constraints. The flow has value $n=|\mathcal K|$ and
cost equal to the negative assignment score.

Conversely, a flow of value $n$ saturates all $n$ unit-capacity
source--vehicle arcs. Conservation and integrality force exactly one
outgoing action per vehicle; generated edges encode feasibility and
action--sink capacities enforce the shared limits. Remaining arc flows
are uniquely determined by these choices, giving a bijection.

The conservation matrix is a directed node--arc incidence matrix and
is totally unimodular. Unit rows for bounds preserve this property.
Integer supplies and bounds therefore make every vertex of the
bounded feasible flow polytope integral. If the LP is feasible, its
linear objective attains an integral optimum and hence a feasible
binary assignment. If either model is infeasible, so is the other.
Private outside actions can ensure feasibility, but their absence does
not invalidate equivalence or integrality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact Capacity-Slack Graph Scaling}
\label{app:proof_additional}
The following batch rule permits vehicles without a certified outside
action. It reduces to the all-outside-action formulation when each
vehicle has an admissible private alternative.

\paragraph{Initialization and deterministic folding.}
Set $\mathcal U^0=\mathcal A$ and initialize
$(b_k^0,\ell_k^0)$ from an explicitly supplied real private action.
Otherwise set $b_k^0=\bot$ and $\ell_k^0=-\infty$. Ordinary encoded
wait, relocation, and station actions remain in $\mathcal U^0$ until
certified; a feasible action is not automatically a private fallback.
The default NYC adapter and ADP-layout benchmark supply no separate
private fallback: admissible wait and relocation enter $\mathcal A$
and become witnesses only through certification. The sparse benchmark
can instead supply explicit private fallback values.
No arithmetic objective coefficient is formed from $-\infty$.
At round $r$, compute
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
B_a^r&=\{k:(k,a)\in\mathcal E_K,\ \Psi_{k,a}>\ell_k^r\},\\
\mathcal D^r&=\{a\in\mathcal U^r:|B_a^r|\le u_a\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:batch_fold_sets}
\end{equation}
If $\mathcal D^r$ is empty, stop. Otherwise each vehicle considers
its incident actions in $\mathcal D^r$ and its old witness. Change the
witness only for a strict score improvement; among equally scoring
improving actions choose the smallest action index. All capacity tests
use $\ell^r$, and all actions in $\mathcal D^r$ are removed together.
This specifies deterministic simultaneous folding, not sequential
recomputation of the certificate within a batch.

\paragraph{Reduced model with forced rows.}
At the fixed point define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal K_B&=\{k:b_k\ne\bot\},\qquad
\mathcal K_F=\mathcal K\setminus\mathcal K_B,\\
c_k&=\begin{cases}\ell_k,&k\in\mathcal K_B,\\0,&k\in\mathcal K_F.\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:baseline_forced_partition}
\end{equation}
Retain
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
E^{\mathrm{red}}=\{(k,a)\in\mathcal E_K:\;&a\in\mathcal U,\\
&k\in\mathcal K_F\ \text{or}\ \Psi_{k,a}>\ell_k\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:general_retained_edges}
\end{equation}
The reduced assignment is
\begin{align}
J_{\mathrm{red}}^\star=\max_z\quad&
\sum_{(k,a)\in E^{\mathrm{red}}}(\Psi_{k,a}-c_k)z_{k,a}
\label{eq:full_reduced_obj}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad&\sum_{a:(k,a)\in E^{\mathrm{red}}}z_{k,a}\le1
&&k\in\mathcal K_B,\label{eq:full_reduced_vehicle}\\
&\sum_{a:(k,a)\in E^{\mathrm{red}}}z_{k,a}=1
&&k\in\mathcal K_F,\label{eq:forced_reduced_vehicle}\\
&\sum_{k:(k,a)\in E^{\mathrm{red}}}z_{k,a}\le u_a
&&a\in\mathcal U,\label{eq:full_reduced_capacity}\\
&z_{k,a}\in\{0,1\}&&(k,a)\in E^{\mathrm{red}}.
\label{eq:full_reduced_binary}
\end{align}
An unmatched vehicle in $\mathcal K_B$ executes its stored witness.
A vehicle in $\mathcal K_F$ must execute a retained real action, even
if its score is negative. Its $c_k=0$ is an objective reference, not
a zero-score executable fallback.

\begin{lemma}[Outside-action ownership invariant]
\label{lem:outside_invariant}
Baseline scores are nondecreasing. If action $a$ is folded at round
$r$, then at every later round $s>r$,
\begin{equation}
\{k:b_k^s=a\}\subseteq B_a^r,\qquad
|\{k:b_k^s=a\}|\le u_a.
\label{eq:outside_owners}
\end{equation}
All stored non-null witnesses, and any subset of them, are jointly
capacity-feasible and use no surviving shared resource.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Strict improvement makes each baseline nondecreasing, including
transitions from $-\infty$. A new owner of $a$ must belong to
$B_a^r$, whose size is at most $u_a$. After that batch $a$ is never
considered again; an owner can keep $a$ or switch away, but no new
owner can acquire it. Initially the supplied private witnesses are
mutually nonconflicting; null markers consume nothing. At later
rounds, each folded resource obeys the ownership bound, private
witnesses remain unique, and each vehicle owns at most one witness.
Deleting witness assignments can only reduce these resource loads.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Domination and forced-row retention]
\label{lem:folded_domination}
A vehicle's final baseline dominates every available initial private
score and every incident folded resource. A surviving nonretained
edge has score at most that vehicle's finite final baseline. Every
vehicle in $\mathcal K_F$ retains all its feasible incident edges.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each folding update takes the maximum of the old baseline and all
incident folded scores, after which the baseline cannot decrease.
A finite-score folded edge incident to a vehicle with baseline
$-\infty$ necessarily gives it a witness. Such a vehicle cannot belong
to $\mathcal K_F$ at termination. Nor is any edge of $\mathcal K_F$
removed by dominance. For other surviving discarded edges the
inequality follows from the retention test.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:exact_scaling}]
Every nonterminal batch removes a resource, so at most $|\mathcal A|$
batches occur. Lift a feasible reduced solution by keeping its chosen
shared edges and assigning the stored witness to each unmatched
vehicle in $\mathcal K_B$. Forced rows already have exactly one
action. Shared choices satisfy the capacities of $\mathcal U$;
witnesses use only disjoint removed or private resources and satisfy
Lemma~\ref{lem:outside_invariant}. The lifted assignment is feasible
and has score
\begin{equation}
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_B}\ell_k+
\sum_{(k,a)\in E^{\mathrm{red}}}(\Psi_{k,a}-c_k)z_{k,a}.
\label{eq:unfolded_score_identity}
\end{equation}
For an optimum this gives
\begin{equation}
J_F^\star\ge\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_B}\ell_k+J_{\mathrm{red}}^\star.
\label{eq:scaling_lower_direction}
\end{equation}

Conversely, take any original feasible assignment $\widehat z$ and keep only its
selected retained edges. Resource loads cannot increase. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:folded_domination}, no selected edge of a forced row
is discarded, so all exactly-one constraints on $\mathcal K_F$
remain satisfied. For every other discarded selection the final
witness score is at least its old score. Thus the resulting reduced
solution $z'$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
J_F(\widehat z)&\le\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_B}\ell_k+
\sum_{(k,a)\in E^{\mathrm{red}}}(\Psi_{k,a}-c_k)z'_{k,a}\\
&\le\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_B}\ell_k+J_{\mathrm{red}}^\star.
\end{split}
\label{eq:scaling_upper_direction}
\end{equation}
The two constructions establish feasibility in both directions,
including simultaneous infeasibility. For feasible instances, taking
the original optimum and combining the inequalities proves the
identity and optimal unfolding. SSG preserves the optimum value and
a recoverable optimum, not every feasible assignment or tied optimum.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why strict ties and genuine outside actions matter.}
Two vehicles with private zero-score alternatives and one shared
capacity-one action of score zero have improving degree zero at that
action. Replacing both old witnesses on a tie would violate its
capacity. Keeping old witnesses on equality prevents this error.
Among genuinely improving ties, the smallest action index makes the
batch and pointer implementations select the same witness.

Feasible charging is weaker than a certified outside option. Consider
two must-charge vehicles and two reachable capacity-one stations, with
both vehicles incident to both stations and no admissible wait. A
complete charging assignment exists, yet each station has two contenders
and neither can be folded initially. Both vehicles correctly remain
in $\mathcal K_F$. With only one such station the instance is
infeasible. A synthetic benchmark that supplies wait to every vehicle
is an all-outside-action special case; the NYC low-SoC wait mask is not.

\paragraph{Equivalence of accelerated preprocessing.}
The implementation initializes improving degrees $g_a^r=|B_a^r|$
and decrements them when edges become dominated. For each action
surviving the next batch, let $\Delta_a^r$ contain its incident
vehicles satisfying $\ell_k^r<\Psi_{k,a}\le\ell_k^{r+1}$. Then
\begin{equation}
g_a^{r+1}=g_a^r-|\Delta_a^r|.
\label{eq:incremental_degree_identity}
\end{equation}
Monotonicity makes this exactly the recomputed degree. Batchwise row
maxima and minimum-index tie selection reproduce the logical folding
rule. Values above the largest updated baseline cannot be dominated;
skipping their row tests is a valid bound, not candidate pruning.

At most four vectorized batches are attempted. If a fifth foldable
batch exists, the implementation \emph{restarts} the monotone-pointer
routine from the original initial state and returns its complete
fixed point. It neither stops after four mathematical rounds nor
continues from a partially transferred fourth-round state. The pointer
routine sorts each row by score and action index, processes batch
actions in increasing index order, and crosses each newly dominated
edge at most once. The same certificates, strict updates, and tie
rule therefore yield the same final witnesses, baselines, surviving
resources, and logical round count. Preallocated arc arrays and lazy
metadata change storage and decoding cost, not the retained network.

\paragraph{Graph size, preprocessing, and solver cost.}
Let $m=|\mathcal A|$ and let $e$ count feasible edges to $\mathcal A$
after zero-capacity removal; $d_k$ is vehicle $k$'s degree in this
edge set. The at most $n$ separate private edges contribute $O(n)$.
The full and reduced networks have $O(n+m)$ vertices and
$O(e+n+m)$ arcs. The current dense-input implementation has
preprocessing time
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
O\!\left(nm+\sum_k d_k\log(1+d_k)+e+n\right.\\
\left.{}+m\log(1+m)\right)
\end{split}
\label{eq:ssg_preprocessing_bound}
\end{equation}
and space $O(nm+e+n+m)$. Dense validation, quantization, and candidate
extraction cost $O(nm)$. A constant number of vectorized batches
costs $O(e+n+m)$. On a deep cascade, row sorting costs
$O(\sum_k d_k\log(1+d_k))$; incident-edge visits and monotone-pointer
updates cost $O(e+n+m)$, and sorting disjoint batches costs at most
$O(m\log(1+m))$. Restarting after four batches preserves this bound.
The dense score and integer matrices remain allocated: this is not
an $O(e)$ end-to-end implementation. Reconstruction is $O(n)$ after
selected edges are identified.

The solver receives $n$ units of supply. It adds a direct vehicle--sink
baseline arc only for $k\in\mathcal K_B$; all other rows must route
their unit through a real retained resource. Hence no private dummy
may be added for $\mathcal K_F$. OR-Tools
\texttt{SimpleMinCostFlow}, used in the measurements, implements
cost-scaling push--relabel. With $N_f$ flow nodes, $A_f$ arcs and
maximum absolute integer cost $C_{\max}$, its documented bound is
$O(N_f^2 A_f\log(N_f\max\{1,C_{\max}\}))$.\footnote{OR-Tools 9.14
implementation: \url{https://github.com/google/or-tools/blob/v9.14/ortools/graph/min_cost_flow.h}.}
For comparison, a successive-shortest-path solver initialized on the
layered acyclic graph with potentials and a binary heap admits
$O(n(e+n+m)\log(n+m))$; this is a separate algorithmic bound, not
the measured OR-Tools implementation. SSG can precede either backend
or a continuous network LP solver such as CPLEX. No strict graph-size
or runtime improvement is guaranteed. End-to-end assignment timing
must include quantization, reduction, graph construction, backend
model creation, optimization, decoding, and per-solve feasibility and
objective verification. Cross-method comparisons and archive writing
are outside this timing scope.

\paragraph{Scope and physical action identity.}
A folded station action retains its station, arrival, service window,
and executable identity. Scores and capacities stay fixed throughout
folding; recomputing GAT embeddings after node deletion would change
the optimized instance. Rerun reduction when state, scores, or
capacities change. The theorem is exact for the encoded independent
resource quotas. It does not certify an outer heuristic that deletes
time-conflicting charging edges, a general interval-scheduling model,
Top-$K$ candidate truncation, or optimality of additive learned values.
